How the model works

  1. Scenario A (pay now): your balance drops by the lump sum before 1 June, and the money is gone. Voluntary repayments are not refundable (ATO).
  2. Scenario B (keep): the lump sum stays in savings or investments, earning your after-tax return each year.
  3. Each year, in this order: indexation is added on 1 June, then the compulsory repayment for that income year is subtracted (calculated from the ATO marginal rates, with thresholds grown at your assumption).
  4. Both scenarios pay the same out of your income each year. Once a scenario's debt is fully repaid, any compulsory repayment it no longer has to make is added to its savings. This keeps the comparison fair.
  5. Result: net position = savings − remaining HELP debt at the end, compared between A and B.

Rule of thumb from the maths: paying early wins when indexation is higher than your after-tax return. The gap is biggest when you would have cleared the debt soon anyway, because each year of indexation you avoid is a real saving.

What this does not model

  • The 11-month rule: only debt older than 11 months is indexed. Enter just that part as the indexed balance.
  • Exact timing: payments must be processed by the ATO before 1 June, and compulsory repayments are only credited when your tax return is assessed. Here they are applied once a year.
  • Home-loan borrowing power, the value of keeping an emergency fund (you can't get the money back), overseas repayment rules, debt cancellation on death, and future law changes such as another one-off reduction.
  • Investment risk: returns are treated as certain.
  • Tax on investment returns, except to the extent you enter an after-tax rate.

Rules used (last checked 2 Nov 2026)

  • 2026–27 compulsory repayment: nil up to $69,528; 15c per $1 over $69,528 up to $129,717; $9,028 plus 17c per $1 over $129,717 up to $186,050; 10% of total repayment income from $186,051. ATO
  • Indexation: applied 1 June to debt older than 11 months, at the lower of CPI and WPI. The 2026 rate was 2.8%. ATO, Study Assist
  • Voluntary repayments: can be made at any time, are not refundable, and do not reduce your compulsory repayment. Paying before 1 June means less indexation, so allow processing time. ATO, Study Assist
  • The "20%": a one-off 20% reduction was applied to debts that existed on 1 June 2025, and the ATO has finished processing it. It is not an ongoing bonus. The old 5% bonus on voluntary repayments ended on 1 Jan 2017. Dept of Education, Parliament
